## Priority 1 — Framing Repair (the post-mortem's fatal flaw)

### P1.1 Retitle the paper

### P1.2 Rewrite the abstract

Replace the entire abstract block in `CSR_Paper.tex` with this exact text (180 words). Do not paraphrase — paste verbatim:

```
Multi-agent debate frameworks let language models critique and revise one another, often improving aggregate performance on reasoning tasks. When agents differ in capability, peer exposure exerts two simultaneous forces on a strong focal agent: a correction signal from peers who got different questions right, and a corruption signal from peers who hold incorrect positions. This work presents a controlled five-condition experiment that varies the number of weak peers exposed to a strong focal agent, using DeepSeek-v4-flash as the primary focal model and GPT-4o-mini for cross-model comparison, across 300 MMLU-Pro and GSM8K questions with five replications per question. For DeepSeek-v4-flash the correction signal dominates: Round 1 accuracy rises from 76.2\% solo to 84.1\% with two weak peers (+7.9 pp). For GPT-4o-mini the corruption signal dominates: accuracy falls 4.0 pp under one weak peer, with a correct-to-incorrect flip rate of 17.2\% under two. Confidence-weighted peer filtering fails as a mitigation because weak agents over-report certainty in 99.5\% of trials. Debate protocols should weight peers by verifiable accuracy, not self-reported confidence.
```

### P1.7 Reconcile trial-level vs. question-level inconsistency

The McNemar test (question-level, 33 worse vs. 12 better, p = 0.0025) appears to contradict the trial-level Δ = +7.9 pp. Both can be true; the paper must say so explicitly.

Insert this paragraph in Section 4.1, immediately after the existing McNemar sentence:
```
These two aggregations measure different objects. The trial-level R1 accuracy (84.1\%) reflects per-trial correctness across 1{,}500 trials; the McNemar test uses per-question majority-vote correctness across 300 questions. The contrast --- a net trial-level gain alongside a net question-level loss --- indicates that the C4 gains concentrate on a subset of questions while a different subset regresses, and that the question-level result is sensitive to majority-vote aggregation over five replications. We treat the trial-level figure as the primary outcome and the McNemar result as a robustness check that surfaces question-level heterogeneity.
```

## Priority 3 — Address Methodological Gaps Without New Experiments

Add the following limitations as a single paragraph at the end of Section 5 (Discussion):

```
Several limitations bound these claims. First, no self-consistency baseline was run; some fraction of the C2 lift (three smart agents debating) may be attributable to sample aggregation rather than to debate-induced revision, and comparing C2 to a self-consistency control is the most important follow-up. Second, in C2 and C3 the smart non-focal agents are also DeepSeek-v4-flash instances, so the C2 result conflates debate-based revision with intra-model self-consistency; a heterogeneous-smart-agent control would isolate the two. Third, MMLU-Pro and GSM8K appear in the pretraining corpora of contemporary LLMs, so the baseline accuracies may overstate genuine reasoning; the relative comparison across conditions is robust to a constant contamination offset, but the 45-percentage-point capability gap should be read as an empirical observation on these specific items. Fourth, the design provides only three peer-count values and only unanimous-wrong peer signals, so the bandwagon mechanism is not isolated from generic distractor pollution. Fifth, both focal models are accessed via commercial APIs, so the mechanistic attribution to sycophancy and the bandwagon effect is supported by the flip-rate patterns but cannot be verified at the representation level.
```

This single paragraph replaces any existing limitations text and addresses the post-mortem's missing-baseline, confound, and contamination concerns by acknowledgement.
