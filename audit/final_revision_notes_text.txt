Explanation of Revisions
Resubmission of ARR submission 1656:

Summary
We thank the three reviewers and the Area Chair. The meta-review’s decision text identified the crux precisely:
“Although the authors stated in their response that additional model runs have been completed,
in-cycle ACL policies reserve these for future revisions, leaving the current paper scope undersupported.”
Every experiment the reviewers asked for had been run but could not be reported in an author response.
This revision puts all of them in the manuscript. The follow-up experiments reuse the same core question
pool, deterministic seed framework and evaluation pipeline, with the condition-specific prompt changes each
requested control requires. Three further experiments go beyond what was asked.
What is new in the manuscript.
1. A solo re-answering control (C1R) — the causal baseline sTG4 and the AC identified as the central
methodological gap. Section 4.2.
2. An honest-peer control (C3H, C4H) — the same weak models answering naturally, addressing
Tf6M’s and sTG4’s objection that persona-anchored peers are adversarial distractors rather than natural
low-capability output. Section 4.2.
3. A confidence-filter re-run with confidence actually elicited (C5R, C5H) plus a corrected pre-flight
test — the “properly designed follow-up” Tf6M asked for. Section 3.4, Section 4.4.
4. An eight-model capability sweep — the systematic sweep including open-weight models that Tf6M
argued was necessary to show a general phenomenon rather than a pairwise anomaly. Section 4.3,
Table 4 and Appendix Figure 2.
5. GPT-4o-mini extended from 50 to the full 300-question pool, answering gL73’s concern about
limited questions.
6. A contamination probe on numerically perturbed GSM8K.
7. A controlled split-peer condition (C4split) — tests whether a visible correct peer changes harmful
revision, removing the last “deferred to future work” in the previous version. Section 4.6.
8. A heterogeneous-smart debate control (C2het) — three architecturally distinct strong models,
converting the “homogeneous debate is close to sampling” limitation into a result.

9. An output-distribution probe on an open-weight focal model, measuring the probability mass that
moves onto the peer-asserted wrong answer between rounds — evidence beneath the discrete choice.
We call it an output-distribution probe, not a mechanism: it observes output probabilities and does not
identify a representation-level cause.
What is new in the writing. The term focal model is now defined at first use in both the abstract and
Section 1 (gL73 W1); Section 1 is restructured around three explicit research questions and the contributions
each end with a pointer to the section, table or figure that supports it (gL73 W2, W5); the conditions are given
in a table rather than in prose; and Section 2.2 gains a paragraph on debate as an error detector, the specific
prior-work area the meta-review named as under-discussed (M7, gL73 W4).
Corrections we made to the previous version. In preparing this revision we re-derived every number in
the manuscript directly from the released per-trial logs, and corrected fourteen discrepancies that survived the
reviewed version. The full list is in the released audit/numerical_consistency_report.md; the
ones that change a reported number are:
• Table 3 previously reported raw McNemar p-values under a caption stating they were Holm-corrected.
Both are now reported in separate columns. The principal conclusions reported in the revised manuscript
are unchanged after the corrected multiplicity procedure.
• The contamination-probe contrast was reported as p = 0.07 without noting that this question-level
test rests on eight discordant pairs. Section 4.5 now states plainly that the probe is underpowered at
the question level (p = 0.23 after correction) while significant at the trial level, and no longer claims
significance for it.
• Appendix S1’s trial-count table still carried the superseded 50-question GPT-4o-mini numbers, which
contradicted Table 2. All data tables are now generated programmatically from the released logs by a
released script, so they cannot drift from the data again.
• Appendix S7 asserted that the weak models’ unprompted accuracy was “not measured in this study”.
The honest-peer conditions measure it directly; the numbers are now given.
• The released corrected-gate report substituted zero for P (loud | correct) on the wrong-anchored
substrate, where that class is empty by construction. This produced an apparent discriminative gap of
0.99 and a spurious “passed” verdict in the artefact, contradicting the paper’s own table. The report
now records the substrate as undefined and gives per-class counts.
• The capability-sweep Spearman ρ was computed on the 1-dp rounded table values, which tie four midrange models together and inflated it to −0.97 with p = 0.0001. On unrounded rates it is ρ = −0.95
with exact permutation p = 0.0011.
• Persona validation counts in Appendix S5 were each off by 14 (the failures were double-counted): the
correct split is 1,116 passing first, 370 after regeneration, 14 failing.
• The primary focal model was described as being of the DeepSeek-V3 family with a citation to that
technical report. The API resolves the requested alias to the served snapshot deepseek-v4-flash,
for which no such report exists, so the manuscript now reports the served snapshot recorded in the run
metadata and makes no architectural claim.

Beyond what was requested
Three experiments in this revision were not asked for. They close the two weaknesses the previous version
conceded in its own Limitations, and add output-distribution evidence.
• Split-peer control (C4split), 1,500 trials. The reviewed version admitted its design could not separate
“a wrong answer is present” from “every visible peer is wrong”, and deferred a split-peer control
to future work. It is now run: one wrong-anchored and one correct-anchored peer, same pool and
replications. Adding a correct peer lowers harmful revision (4.8% → 3.4% paired, n = 1,042,
p = 0.03) and raises accuracy to 86.3% — +4.7 points over two wrong peers (p = 0.013 corrected)
and +11.7 over solo — while the correction rate is unchanged (56.0% vs 55.8%). We initially read
this as evidence that unanimity drives the harm, then tested that directly and found it false: within C4
the two anchored peers name the same wrong answer in only 16.4% of trials, and whether they agree
makes no difference to harmful revision (5.95% vs 6.58%, p = 0.88). The manuscript therefore claims
only that a visible correct peer reduces harmful revision, and states that C4split changes two things at
once (removes a wrong peer, adds a correct one) so it isolates neither.
• Heterogeneous-smart debate (C2het), 900 trials. The reviewed version conceded that its homogeneous condition was closer to self-consistency sampling than to debate (pairwise κ = 0.72, ESS ≈ 1.2).
Three architecturally distinct strong models now debate on the same pool. This heterogeneous control
did not improve performance: accuracy is indistinguishable from solo (+0.7, p = 0.87), the focal ends
below where it started (79.0% → 77.3%) where self-sampling gains (75.8% → 78.7%), and harmful
6

revision rises from 3.3% to 5.8% (n = 638, p = 0.02). Because both distinct peers are also 9–10
points weaker than the focal, diversity and peer strength are not separated and the control does not
estimate the effect of diversity alone; the manuscript says so explicitly and keeps the corresponding
limitation.
• Output-distribution probe. Limitations previously said the study was “behavioural, not mechanistic”.
On an open-weight focal model served locally (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, 2,700 trials) we measure the
probability mass that moves onto the peer-asserted wrong answer between rounds. Homogeneous
peers move the model away from that answer (−0.018); two confidently wrong peers move it toward it
(+0.064). Paired by (question, replication) the shift is +0.082 (95% CI [+0.067, +0.097], Wilcoxon
p ≈ 10−42 , n = 898). The pull is therefore present and measurable on trials where the stated answer
never changes — which flip rates structurally cannot show.
A matched observational comparison inside the honest control. Among matched questions where both
honest peers happened to be wrong, naturally occurring weak-peer errors were associated with a higher
harmful-revision rate than persona-anchored errors (29.7% against 14.4%, paired by question, p < 10−5 ),
and this persists when stratified on whether the two peers named the same wrong answer. Because the
comparison conditions on peers happening to err, and the two kinds of message differ in style, it does not
identify whether the anchored design over- or under-estimates deployment risk. We report it as an association,
and it speaks directly to Tf6M W2 and sTG4 W1 on the difference between instructed distractors and natural
weak output.

Reviewer and meta-reviewer preference
We request the same reviewers and the same Area Chair. Every point raised is implemented in the
manuscript rather than promised, on the identical pool, seed and protocol, and the returning reviewers are best
placed to judge whether the prior weaknesses are resolved. The title is unchanged so the paper is recognisable.
